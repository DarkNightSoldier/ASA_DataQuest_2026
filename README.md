## The question that started it

In 2023, **130** of the 237 countries and areas had fewer children than needed to replace their parents.
Yet **84** of them were still growing: **66** because births exceed deaths, **18** only because more people arrive than
leave. One number cannot explain that. We ask three questions and answer each one in a sentence
(source: `slides/script/narration_en.md`; every number comes from `figures/numbers.json`).

1. **Which lever moves each country's population (births, deaths or migration), and does it differ by region and
   income?** Child deaths started falling first and births followed (in 106 of 109 high-fertility countries), each country
   at its own pace and latest in low-income countries, while migration moves on its own and weighs most in high-income
   countries, where it accounts for about seven in ten of the growth since 2000. This is a sequence in time, not proof of cause.
2. **Does one indicator tell the whole story?** No: fertility alone captures about three quarters of the differences in how
   old populations are, but only part of today's growth and almost none of migration, so the story emerges only when
   transition and migration are read together (the Emirates and Belarus have the same fertility; one grows almost four
   percent a year, the other shrinks).
3. **Do countries share one road at different speeds, and what should the UN watch?** Mostly yes: grouped by their
   seventy-year paths, countries form three stretches of one road plus a migration-led group (mostly Gulf states), and the
   UN should watch child survival where it is off pace for the 2030 goal, ageing where fertility is low but populations still
   grow, and migration data where growth depends on arrivals.

## How to reproduce everything, step by step

Run all commands from the repository root. Setup of R 4.6.1, the Python venv and Node: `SETUP.md`.

```bash
# 0. Python venv used for the notebook, the PDF and the video (Chromium: /usr/bin/chromium)
python3 -m venv .venv
.venv/bin/pip install jupyter nbconvert nbclient nbformat beautifulsoup4 imageio-ffmpeg playwright pypdf
.venv/bin/playwright install ffmpeg
export VENV=$PWD/.venv            # video/*.sh read this variable
# the R kernel for Jupyter is registered once with IRkernel::installspec(user = TRUE) (see SETUP.md)

# 1. Raw data in the PARENT folder of the repo (we used the WPP 2024 files given by the DataQuest organisers,
#    timestamps 2026-10-02; you can download them from the UN, see data/raw/README.md, and compare the SHA-256 in data/README.md)
#    ../WPP2024_Demographic_Indicators_Medium_csv.gz  ·  ../WPP2024_Supplement_Births_and_LE60.csv

# 2. R packages, clean data and tests
Rscript R/00_instalar_paquetes.R            # idempotent: installs only what is missing
Rscript R/01_preparar_datos.R               # countries vs aggregates, regions, income groups
Rscript R/tests_datos.R && Rscript R/tests_incertidumbre.R

# 3. Analysis, one script per question (seed 2026; ≈ 9–12 min, 2–3 min and 3 min)
Rscript R/02_q1_palancas.R
Rscript R/03_q2_un_numero.R
Rscript R/04_q3_caminos.R

# 4. Single source of numbers (385 keys, 2 of them metadata: repo and meta_human_check; those checked by independent code carry "verified": true)
Rscript R/05_numbers_json.R                 # writes figures/numbers.json

# 5. Notebook (≈ 14 min to run end to end)
$VENV/bin/python -I notebook/construir_notebook.py
PATH=$VENV/bin:$PATH jupyter nbconvert --to notebook --execute --inplace \
    --ExecutePreprocessor.timeout=3600 notebook/analisis_wpp2024.ipynb
$VENV/bin/python -I notebook/exportar_html.py        # notebook/analisis_wpp2024.html with alt text

# 6. Final figures (PNG in figures/static/, MP4 in figures/animated/; FIG_SIN_MP4=1 skips the MP4)
for s in fig_q1_slide1 fig_q1_slide2 fig_q2_slide3 fig_q3_slide4; do Rscript R/$s.R; done

# 7. Slides, script and PDF
(cd slides && npm install)                                   # reveal.js and flag-icons (node_modules is not versioned)
$VENV/bin/python slides/script/build_narration.py            # narration, captions, timings (only verified numbers)
$VENV/bin/python slides/script/fill_numbers.py --check-html slides/index.html   # lists missing/unverified keys
$VENV/bin/python slides/dev/check_sync.py                   # real time, ≈ 5 min: captions = voice, 32 steps in order, MP4 timings
bash video/export_pdf.sh --png                               # slides/export/slides.pdf (checks it has 6 pages)
bash slides/abrir.sh                                         # browse the deck (or double-click slides/index.html)

# 8. Video: silent deck video, then the team's voice, then the final MP4 (≤ 300 s is checked)
$VENV/bin/python video/record_slides.py --mp4                # video/deck_silent.mp4 (≈ 70 MB, not versioned)
#   record the voice reading slides/script/narration_en.md → video/voz.wav (see video/README.md §2)
bash video/assemble.sh video/voz.wav                         # video/final/UNALSta-R_DataQuest2026.mp4
```

The recorder refuses to run if a number on screen has no `"verified": true` (rule from `docs/QA_stats.md`).

## Ethics: ASA guidelines, limitations and AI-use statement

We follow the ASA *Ethical Guidelines for Statistical Practice* (2022), https://doi.org/10.5281/zenodo.7092386. Codes in brackets point to the guideline that motivates each choice. The checklist and its answers: `docs/04_etica_ASA_aplicada.md`, `docs/QA_ethics.md`, `docs/qa_etica.csv`.

- **Data [B.1, D.10].** UN DESA, Population Division (2024), *World Population Prospects 2024* (CC BY 3.0 IGO), original July 2024 file. 1950–2023 = UN model-based estimates; 2024–2100 = UN medium projection, a scenario, always in grey. The UN's January 2026 interim update for Togo is not applied (`docs/correcciones.md`, C-46).
- **Weakest component [B.2, C.4].** Net migration (arrivals − departures) is often inferred by the UN from census gaps; its projections rest on expert assumptions.
- **Uncertainty [B.2].** UAE 2050: 15.4 M on the UN medium line, UN 95 % range 8.9–33.3 M; for 77 of 161 countries the 2050 range includes both growth and decline.
- **Countries, not aggregates [B.1].** Each of the 237 countries and areas is counted once; models use the 161 with ≥ 1 M people.
- **Choices that matter [A.2, B.7].** Income groups = World Bank FY2027, applied to all years; lever types use 2023 (15–25 "grow only through migration", depending on the year). All data decisions: `docs/decisiones_de_datos.md`.
- **Exploratory, not causal [B.3, C.3].** Hypotheses were fixed on 2026-10-23 (commit `2a0ab7b`, sha256 `83f98f6e1a3d`) before the final run, but written after exploring the same data; what we did not find: notebook §8.
- **Fair framing [A.8, D.6].** Groups describe demographic paths, not development or worth; UN names and terminology.
- **Examples chosen by rule [A.4].** The three most populous countries (≥ 1 M) of each type or group; full lists in `figures/miembros_*.csv`; Colombia appears as our home country.
- **Privacy [D.4].** No personal data are used; all data are national aggregates published by the UN.
- **AI use [A.5, B.2].** Claude Code (Anthropic) AI agents, directed by the team, assisted the analysis, the code and the production: they wrote most of the R code, the analysis memos, the figures and drafts of the slides and script. They did not make the voices: team members narrate the video and no synthetic voice is used. Every number was recomputed by independent code and checked by the four team members (numbers shown or said; the independent code, also AI-written, starts from the raw UN files: `docs/QA_stats.md`; the hand check was on 2026-10-26 at 11:23 UTC−5: `docs/QA_stats.md` §10). The four members share equal responsibility for the work. Log: `docs/registro_ia.md`; human sign-off of numbers: `docs/verificacion_numeros.csv`.
- **Conflicts of interest [A.7].** None; no funding; not affiliated with the UN or the World Bank.
- **What this project is not [A.1].** Not a UN product, not our own forecast, not a causal study, not a ranking of countries.
- **Corrections [B.5].** `docs/correcciones.md` (the 60 QA findings and how each was handled; the ones not applied are listed there with the reason).

## Objetivo y respuestas

En 2023, **130** de los 237 países y áreas tenían menos hijos de los necesarios para reemplazar a sus padres, y sin embargo
**84** seguían creciendo: **66** porque los nacimientos superan a las muertes y **18** solo porque llega más gente de la que se
va. Un solo número no explica eso. Respondemos tres preguntas, cada una en una frase (fuente: `slides/script/narration_en.md`;
todas las cifras salen de `figures/numbers.json`):

1. **¿Qué palanca mueve la población de cada país (nacimientos, muertes o migración) y cambia según región e ingreso?**
   La mortalidad infantil empezó a bajar primero y los nacimientos la siguieron (en 106 de 109 países de fecundidad alta),
   cada país a su ritmo y más tarde en los de ingreso bajo; la migración se mueve por su cuenta y pesa más en los de ingreso alto,
   donde explica cerca de siete de cada diez del crecimiento desde 2000. Es una secuencia en el tiempo, no una prueba de causa.
2. **¿Un solo indicador cuenta toda la historia?** No: la fecundidad sola capta cerca de tres cuartas partes de las diferencias
   en la edad de las poblaciones, pero solo parte del crecimiento actual y casi nada de la migración; la historia aparece cuando
   se leen juntas la transición y la migración (Emiratos Árabes Unidos y Bielorrusia tienen la misma fecundidad; uno crece casi
   4 % al año y el otro decrece).
3. **¿Los países recorren un mismo camino a distinta velocidad y qué debe vigilar la ONU?** Casi siempre sí: agrupados por sus
   trayectorias de setenta años forman tres tramos de un mismo camino más un grupo guiado por la migración (sobre todo del
   Golfo); la ONU debería vigilar la supervivencia infantil donde va a destiempo de la meta de 2030, el envejecimiento donde la
   fecundidad es baja pero la población aún crece, y los datos de migración donde el crecimiento depende de las llegadas.
